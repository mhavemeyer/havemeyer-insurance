What I help with

Coverage for trucking operations

Every operation is different. These are the coverages I most often review with fleet owners.

Auto liability

Primary liability for bodily injury and property damage caused by your trucks, including the coverage needed to meet federal and state filing requirements.

Physical damage

Collision and comprehensive coverage for your tractors and trailers, so a wreck, theft or storm doesn't take a unit off the road for good.

Motor truck cargo

Protection for the freight you haul against loss or damage in transit, matched to the commodities and values you carry.

General liability

Coverage for claims that don't involve driving, such as incidents at your yard, terminal or a customer's dock.

Workers' compensation

Coverage for employee injuries on the job, required for most employers, with attention to how drivers and yard staff are classified.

Umbrella / excess liability

Additional liability limits above your primary policies for larger claims and for contracts that call for higher limits.

Non-trucking & bobtail liability

Liability options for owner-operators and leased units when a truck is being driven outside of dispatch.

Trailer interchange

Coverage for non-owned trailers in your care under a trailer interchange agreement.

Other coverages

Ask about reefer breakdown, hired and non-owned auto, and other coverages your contracts or customers require.

Safety & CSA

Your safety record is part of your premium

FMCSA's Compliance, Safety, Accountability (CSA) program uses roadside inspection, violation and crash data to measure carrier safety in categories called BASICs, including Unsafe Driving, Hours-of-Service Compliance, Vehicle Maintenance, Driver Fitness, Controlled Substances/Alcohol, Hazardous Materials Compliance and Crash Indicator.

Many insurance underwriters look at that same public safety data when they decide whether to offer coverage and how to price it. A carrier with frequent violations or out-of-service orders can look like a higher risk on paper, even if the business has already fixed the underlying problem.

How I can help

  • Walk through your FMCSA safety data and what an underwriter is likely to notice
  • Point out the BASICs and violation types driving your scores
  • Talk through practical steps: inspections, maintenance records, driver files and hours of service
  • Help you explain your safety improvements when your coverage is up for renewal

Safety guidance is general and educational. It isn't legal advice and doesn't guarantee a change in scores or premiums.

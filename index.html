“Risk doesn't go away. It moves.”

Follow the cost to who holds it. When fuel, insurance or legal costs go up, someone in the chain pays. The fleets that do best price on their real cost, keep a complete file, and use safety and operations to lower their total cost of risk.

By the numbers

Figures that shape cash, coverage and credit

These figures come from my 2027 U.S. Trucking Risk Outlook and the research it cites. Each one has a named source. Together they describe the conditions middle-market fleets and companies with fleets are operating in: rising insurance costs, thin margins, more litigation, and more pressure on cash and credit.

+18.2%

Liability premiums per mile rose 18.2% from 2021 to 2024, while crash involvement fell 2.6%.

Source: ATRI, “New ATRI Research Analyzes Motor Carrier Responses to Rising Insurance Costs,” May 2026.

0.6%

Refrigerated carriers averaged a 0.6% operating margin in 2025, and flatbed carriers lost money.

Source: ATRI, “An Analysis of the Operational Costs of Trucking: 2026 Update.”

+12%

U.S. business insolvencies are forecast to rise 12% in 2026, to 27,700 cases.

Source: Allianz Research, Global Insolvencies Outlook, Oct. 27, 2026.

$4.0M

The median truck-crash award is $4.0 million in state courts versus $550,000 in federal courts, 2023–2026.

Source: U.S. Chamber Institute for Legal Reform, Oct. 2026.

84%

On average, 84% of nuclear-verdict awards are non-economic or punitive damages.

Source: ATRI, “Critical Issues in the Trucking Industry – 2026” (citing ATRI Trucking Litigation, Dec. 2025).

$604M

A $604 million broker verdict was upheld in October 2026. In May 2026, the Supreme Court ruled 9–0 in Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II that brokers can be sued in state court for negligent hiring.

Source: FreightWaves, Oct. 16, 2026, and May 14, 2026.

+30.8%

The average freight invoice rose 30.8% to $2,210 at a large freight-payment and factoring firm.

Source: Triumph Financial, via Cobalt Intelligence, Oct. 22, 2026.

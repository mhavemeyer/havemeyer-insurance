2027 U.S. Trucking Risk Outlook

Research by Michael Havemeyer, TRS, TCS · November 2026 · Summary of the 22-page report

Refrigerated, bulk, heavy haul and building materials/construction fleets, private and for-hire: what the economy, the insurance market, the courts and the regulators mean for your cash, your coverage and your credit. Covers all 50 states and Washington, D.C., in five regions.

Risk doesn't go away. It moves.

Every week in my newsletter I follow the same few forces: fuel and freight, the insurance market, the courts, and the rules coming out of Washington and the state capitols. Each one hits a fleet's bank account. Together they decide who has cash at the end of the month, who can get insured, and who can pay their bills. This outlook puts those forces in one place for 2027. It is written for owners, safety leaders and finance managers at fleets across the country, and for the shippers and contractors who depend on them. Use it as proof in rate talks, and as a reminder that your own effort pays.

The thread through this outlook. Follow the cost to who holds it. When fuel, insurance or legal costs go up, someone in the chain pays. Too often it is the small fleet, which acts as everyone's shock absorber. The fleets that do best price on their real cost, keep a complete file, and use safety and operations to lower their total cost of risk.

1. Economy and freight: higher rates are not the same as more freight

Freight bills are getting bigger. That is mostly because costs went up, not because there is much more to haul. Total freight spending rose 20.4% in a year while shipments rose 1.9% (Cass, Sept. 2026). Margins were already thin before this year's fuel spike: in 2025, refrigerated carriers kept about 0.6 cents of each revenue dollar, dry van truckload 0.4 cents, and flatbed and oversize carriers lost money (ATRI).

  • A bigger freight bill does not mean a healthier fleet. Track profit per mile, not revenue.
  • Make sure fuel surcharges reset often enough to keep up with $6 diesel.
  • Your customers are paying more too. Some will push back on rates or pay slower. Plan for it.
  • Fuel and tax costs differ a lot by region. Price each lane on the cost where it runs.

2. Insurance market: a more selective market, splitting by fleet quality

Commercial auto results are getting better for insurers. That does not mean every fleet gets a break. The CIAB broker survey found average commercial auto increases of 4.5% in Q2 2026, with umbrella and excess up 5.3%. But the market is splitting: a broker forecast for the first half of 2026 put fleets under 200 units with good loss history at flat to +7.5%, and similar fleets with poor losses at +20% to +40% (USI). Another wholesale broker says pricing for a weak account can be up to five times higher than for a strong one (RPS). Averages hide a wide spread. Your result depends on your losses, your data and your size.

The federal safety net is not your coverage. Most interstate for-hire carriers have a federal form on their policy called the MCS-90. It makes the insurer pay a final court judgment to an injured member of the public, even if the truck is not listed on the policy. But the fleet agrees to pay the insurer back for anything the policy itself would not have covered (FMCSA, Form MCS-90).

  • Start renewal work 90 to 120 days out. A complete, clean submission is your best price lever.
  • Show your safety data and what you did with it, not just that you own cameras.
  • Report every new driver and truck to your agent before the first trip, and remove the ones who leave.
  • Ask about every market that fits, including RRGs and tech-based insurers. Ask who backs the policy and whether your customers accept it.
  • Expect excess and umbrella layers to cost more and take longer to fill.

3. Nuclear verdicts: the cost of a crash is set in the courtroom

Crash rates did not rise. Payouts did. From 2021 to 2024, truck crash rates fell 2.6%, but liability losses per mile rose 33.1%, and premiums rose 18.6% (ATRI). Where a case is heard matters: the median truck crash award was $4.0 million in state courts but about $550,000 in federal courts, 2023–2026 (U.S. Chamber Institute for Legal Reform).

The liability chain got longer. In May 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 9–0 in Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II that brokers can be sued in state court for negligent hiring of carriers. In October a Texas judge upheld a $604 million verdict against a large broker; the carrier it hired had a Satisfactory rating (FreightWaves). Brokers and shippers will vet carriers harder, ask for higher limits and push more risk back onto the carrier through contracts.

Your own data matters most. Trucking defense attorney Doug Marcello calls it the "Discovery Fallacy": the idea that if you don't look at your data, it can't hurt you. ELD, telematics, video and phone records exist either way and can be requested in a lawsuit. His advice: "Don't fear your data. Use it."

  • Expect brokers and shippers to demand more: higher limits, better scores, tighter contracts.
  • Treat every safety alert as a record that will be read later. Act on it and write down what you did.
  • Work with your own counsel on how data reviews are set up.

4. Litigation funding: this cannot be ignored

Hedge funds, other investors and even foreign wealth funds now pay for lawsuits in return for a share of the verdict or settlement. Truck crashes have become a place to invest, and others profit from the losses. Swiss Re found $17 billion went into litigation funding worldwide in 2020, more than half of it in the U.S. Funded litigated auto injury claims lasted 9.6 times longer than those without, and had 361% higher total losses (U.S. Chamber Institute for Legal Reform, citing Sedgwick). It does not even help plaintiffs much: research cited by ATRI found plaintiffs keep about 43% of payouts in funded cases, versus 55% without. More than 20 states now regulate litigation funding, and North Carolina banned it outright in 2026 (Triple-I).

  • Plan as if a serious crash claim may be backed by investors. Report it fast and keep your records ready, because funded cases tend to last longer.

5. Regulation: paperwork can now park a truck

Some 2026 rule changes cut costs. Others can stop a fleet cold over a filing error. One analysis found more than 6,475 carriers were suspended after insurance requirements changed in FMCSA's Motus registration system (CDLWire, reporting a Central Analysis Bureau and CCJ analysis). A suspended carrier cannot legally run, which means no revenue. Since June 2025, more than 26,000 drivers have been placed out of service for English proficiency (ATRI). Under the new DataQs process, carriers get a three-stage review with set decision times, but the burden of proof is on the person who files (Overdrive). Insurers, brokers and lenders all read your safety record.

  • Check your Motus/registration record and insurance filings every month, and before every renewal.
  • Audit driver files now for English proficiency and CDL status. A lost driver is a lost truck.
  • Challenge wrong inspection or crash data quickly and keep proof.

6. Workforce and theft: fewer drivers, costlier crashes, smarter thieves

People and cargo are where small problems become big claims. Average truck driver turnover was 44.2% in 2025 (ATRI). A study of more than 12,000 drivers at one large carrier found drivers who stayed were about 65% less likely to have a serious crash than drivers who quit (Virginia Tech Transportation Institute / NSTSCE). ATRI found a prior crash raised a driver's chance of another crash by 113%. Thieves stole an estimated $725 million in cargo in 2025, up 60% (Verisk CargoNet).

One crash, several claims. A single serious crash can trigger an auto liability claim, a workers' comp claim for your driver, and damage to your truck and the load. Each one can raise next year's premium. Together they can drain the cash you set aside for deductibles. That is why crash prevention is also cash protection.
  • Never shortcut driver hiring checks, even when trucks sit empty.
  • Track turnover and first-year crashes. Give every new driver a mentor.
  • Confirm any change in pickup details by calling a known number, not the one in the email.

7. The credit connection: it all lands on cash, and on who pays

Every force in this outlook ends up in the same place: a fleet's ability to pay its bills, and its customers' ability to pay theirs. Fuel, insurance and wages rise; margins shrink; invoices get bigger and customers pay slower; there is less cash for deductibles and premiums; and the chance rises that someone cannot pay. Allianz Research expects U.S. insolvencies to rise 12% in 2026, to 27,700, and keep climbing through 2028.

A trucking company acts like a lender to its customers. You pay for fuel, drivers and insurance today, then wait weeks to get paid. When the average freight invoice jumped 30.8% to $2,210 at one large freight payment and factoring company (Cobalt Intelligence, citing Triumph Financial's Q3 2026 filings), each slow payer tied up more of the carrier's cash.

  • Customer concentration. Know what share of revenue your top three customers bring. Ask: could I survive if one stopped paying for 90 days?
  • Slow payers. Track days-to-pay for each customer every month. A customer who slows down is often a warning sign.
  • Know who you are dealing with. Confirm the identity, authority and payment details of new brokers and shippers before you haul or extend terms.
  • Cash for deductibles. If you take a higher deductible or retention to save premium, set aside the cash to pay it.
  • Keep coverage in force. A missed premium payment can cancel a policy and the filings that keep your authority active.

What fleets can do now: Collect. Analyze. Act. Document.

The same closed loop that defends a lawsuit also wins a better renewal and protects cash.

Collect

Keep ELD, telematics, camera and maintenance data in one place. Keep complete driver files. Keep one list of every driver and truck, and match it to your policy each month.

Analyze

Review the top risky behaviors by driver each month. Track days-to-pay and revenue share for your top customers. Track driver turnover and first-year crashes.

Act

Coach drivers within days of an alert, and follow up. Challenge wrong safety data through DataQs. Reset fuel surcharges and rates to real cost.

Document

Date and save every coaching session and fix. Report claims promptly. Build a complete renewal file 90 to 120 days early. Bring your cost per mile and safety record to every rate talk.

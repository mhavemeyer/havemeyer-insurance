What to have ready before your trucking insurance renewal

A practical checklist so your renewal is a conversation about your operation, not a last-minute scramble.

The renewals that go best usually start early. When information comes in at the last minute, underwriters have less time to understand your operation and fewer options are on the table. Here's what I'd suggest pulling together ahead of time.

1. Loss runs

Ask your current and prior insurers for loss runs covering the last several years. Read them yourself. Check for claims that should be closed, reserves that look off, or claims you don't recognize.

2. Driver list

A current list of drivers with license details, experience and hire dates. Be ready for MVRs to be reviewed. Know your hiring standards and how you handle drivers with violations.

3. Equipment schedule

Every tractor and trailer, with year, make, VIN and value. Remove units you've sold and add new ones. Outdated schedules are one of the most common sources of coverage gaps.

4. Operations description

What you haul, typical radius, main lanes and states, and any changes since last year, such as new customers, new commodities or growth in power units. Changes are fine; surprises after binding aren't.

5. Safety program documentation

Written policies on maintenance, inspections, hours of service, drug and alcohol testing, driver training, and any telematics or camera systems, plus evidence that you follow them.

6. Your FMCSA data, reviewed

Look at your inspection and crash history before an underwriter does. If something's inaccurate, file a DataQs request early. If something's accurate but has been fixed, be ready to explain what changed.

7. Contract requirements

Gather the insurance requirements in your shipper, broker and lease agreements, such as specific limits, additional insured status or cargo amounts, so coverage can be matched to what you've actually agreed to.

A note on timing

Starting the conversation well before the expiration date gives everyone room to ask questions and, if needed, explore the market. Last-minute renewals tend to limit choices.

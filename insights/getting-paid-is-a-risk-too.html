Everyone Worries About Freight Rates. Few Worry About Getting Paid.

By Michael Havemeyer, TRS, TCS · LinkedIn posts, about Nov. 5, 2026

Which risk is more likely to put a profitable company out of business? Why receivables, counterparty risk and cash flow belong in every risk conversation.

Everyone worries about freight rates. Few worry about getting paid. Which risk is more likely to put a profitable company out of business?

You did the hard part. You picked up on time. You delivered on time. You sent the invoice. Now you wait.

Cash flow is fragile

When more than £25 million is owed across a supply chain and creditors may absorb losses (a case recently reported by The Produce Wire), the issue isn't just one company's failure. It highlights how fragile cash flow becomes when leverage, financing, and risk assumptions collide with reality.

Trade credit insurance and supply chain finance are no longer optional risk tools; they're strategic necessities.

Regulatory friction lands in working capital

Stronger cabotage enforcement is tightening cross-border trucking capacity just as nearshoring drives more U.S.-Mexico freight demand. When regulatory friction rises, transportation costs, working capital needs, and counterparty risk often follow. That makes total cost of risk, trade credit insurance, and supply chain finance increasingly strategic.

A disconnected economy raises the cost of doing business

A $200 cup of coffee isn't innovation; it's a warning sign that parts of the economy have become disconnected from everyday commercial reality. As asset prices and luxury spending detach from productive activity, transportation, logistics, and SMEs face rising financing, credit, and operating costs. Managing total cost of risk matters more than ever.

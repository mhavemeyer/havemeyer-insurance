DataQs: how to challenge inaccurate inspection and crash data

Your safety data follows you. Here's how FMCSA's review process works when something on your record is wrong.

Because your inspection and crash history affects how enforcement, customers and insurers see your operation, it's worth making sure it's accurate. FMCSA provides a process for that called DataQs.

What DataQs is

DataQs is FMCSA's online system for requesting a review of federal and state data that may be incomplete or incorrect. That includes inspection reports, violations, crash reports and some other records. Requests are usually routed to the state agency that produced the record, since it's the state's data to correct.

What it can and can't do

DataQs is for data that's wrong, not data that's unwelcome. Common examples of valid requests:

  • An inspection or crash assigned to the wrong carrier or USDOT number.
  • A violation recorded that doesn't match what happened, or a duplicate entry.
  • A citation tied to a violation that was later dismissed or resulted in a not-guilty finding in court.

Disagreeing with an officer's judgment, without supporting evidence, is unlikely to succeed.

Crash preventability

For certain types of crashes, FMCSA runs a Crash Preventability Determination Program, where carriers can submit a request through DataQs to have an eligible crash reviewed and marked as not preventable. Check FMCSA's current list of eligible crash types and documentation requirements before filing.

Tips for a stronger request

  • Be specific. Identify the report number, date and exact violation or detail that's incorrect.
  • Attach documentation. Court records, repair invoices, photos, dash camera footage, police reports, bills of lading or lease agreements, whatever directly shows the error.
  • Stay factual and brief. Reviewers handle many requests; a clear explanation helps.
  • File promptly. Records and memories are easier to gather soon after an event.
  • Track your requests. Keep a log of what you filed, when, and the outcome.

Make it part of your routine

Reviewing new inspections as they post, and challenging real errors right away, keeps your record accurate before it matters at a renewal or a customer review.

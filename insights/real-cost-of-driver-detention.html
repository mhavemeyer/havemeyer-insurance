The Real Cost of Driver Detention

By Michael Havemeyer, TRS, TCS · LinkedIn post, about Nov. 6, 2026

Key findings from ATRI's detention research, and why waiting at the dock is a cost, productivity and safety issue.

A report from the American Transportation Research Institute (ATRI) found that driver detention continues to be a major problem for trucking companies, drivers, and the entire supply chain.

Key findings (ATRI)

  • Drivers were detained at 39.3% of all stops in 2023.
  • Detention was even more common for refrigerated carriers (56.2%), women drivers (49.1%), and spot-market freight (42.5%).
  • Depending on the operation, drivers lost 117 to 209 hours per year waiting at customer facilities.
  • Across the trucking industry, detention resulted in over 135 million lost productive hours in 2023.
  • While most carriers charge detention fees, fewer than half of those invoices are paid.
  • The industry lost an estimated $3.6 billion in direct expenses and $11.5 billion in lost productivity due to detention.
  • Trucks that experienced detention drove 14.6% faster on average, suggesting detention may contribute to increased safety risks.

According to Chad England, CEO of C.R. England, as cited in the report, detention has become so common that many carriers accept it as part of doing business, despite its significant impact on costs, productivity, driver satisfaction, and highway safety.

Why it matters

Detention isn't just lost time. It's lost revenue, unpaid invoices, frustrated drivers and, if trucks are making up time on the road, added risk. For more on what fleets can do about it, read "Just in Time to Be Too Late."

2027: What's Really Driving Your Trucking Insurance Bill, and What You Can Control

By Michael Havemeyer, TRS, TCS · November 2026

Crash rates have not been climbing, yet the bill keeps growing. What the new market reports say, in plain words: some of it you cannot control. A lot of it you can.

Every fleet owner I talk to asks the same question at renewal: "Why did my bill go up again?" It is a fair question. Crash rates have not been climbing. Many fleets run cleaner than they did five years ago. Yet the bill keeps growing.

I read a stack of new market reports this fall to find the real answer. Here is what they say, in plain words. Some of it you cannot control. A lot of it you can.

Part 1: What you can't control

The price of a crash keeps rising. Crashes are not the main problem. The cost of each one is. Alera Group says fleets are getting into fewer crashes, but the claims from those crashes "have become drastically more expensive." Repairs are part of it. Insurance Office of America (IOA) reports that truck parts and labor prices rose 27.4% from 2020 to 2025. Medical bills are rising too.

Courts and investors. Lawsuits are where the big money goes. Investors now pay for lawsuits in return for a share of the payout. Alera Group says this money lets plaintiffs "drag out litigation for years." Underwriters now price a policy as if even a simple auto claim could turn into a multimillion-dollar case.

Rate increases have not stopped. The reports do not agree on exactly how much rates are rising, so here are the numbers side by side:

  • The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers (CIAB) survey found commercial auto rates up 4.5% on average in the second quarter of 2026.
  • In the first quarter, that same survey had commercial auto up 5.8%. Risk Placement Services (RPS) notes that was the 59th quarter in a row of increases.
  • Alera Group puts mid-2026 averages at about +8% for commercial auto and +11% for umbrella, and expects double digits for many fleets.

Averages hide a wide spread. RPS says pricing for a weak account can be up to five times higher than for a strong one. That gap is the most important number in this article, because it is the part you can move.

Umbrella limits are shrinking. Alera Group says a $5 million umbrella used to be easy to buy. Now $1 million to $3 million is the norm. If you need more, it often takes several layered policies. That means more insurers, more paperwork and more cost.

Fewer drivers. RPS reports that states had revoked more than 28,000 non-domiciled CDLs by May 2026. These drivers make up about 5% of all CDL holders. Fewer drivers means more pressure to hire fast. Hiring fast is how risk gets in.

Brokers are under the microscope. After the U.S. Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling in Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, brokers can be sued for the carriers they pick. RPS says coverage for brokers is getting harder to buy. Brokers will vet carriers more closely. Clean carriers will win loads. Weak ones may lose them.

Part 2: What you can control

Here is the good news. Underwriters are looking harder at each fleet, not just at averages. That means your own record matters more than ever.

1. Tell a complete story.

Alera Group says most underwriters now use digital tools that grade a submission on how good its data is. Weak submissions "fall to the bottom of the pile." RPS lists what a strong submission includes:

  • Up-to-date driver and truck lists
  • Current loss runs and driving records
  • Any gaps between your federal records and your policy, fixed before you submit
  • What you haul and how far you go
  • Your safety program and telematics data
  • Notes on any large losses, and financials

Start 90 to 120 days before renewal. A complete file is your best price lever.

2. Report every claim, fast.

RPS warns that some fleets with big deductibles skip reporting a small loss. Then the loss grows, and the defense is already hurt. Report every incident right away, even if you think it will stay under your deductible.

3. Be honest about your history.

RPS flags a growing problem: some businesses buy an older DOT number so a new company looks like it has years of history. That can mislead underwriters and lead to misrepresentation concerns. If you are new, say so. Underwriters can work with a young company that runs like a mature one.

4. Hire carefully and know the rules.

IOA cites an analysis of federal data for small fleets that found drivers with an English proficiency problem had a 25% higher crash rate than drivers with a drug or alcohol violation. Check every driver file for English proficiency and CDL status. IOA also notes that recent violations now count more in CSA scores. A bad month hurts faster, but fixes show up faster too.

5. Use your data, and coach on it.

Cameras and telematics are no longer extras. Alera Group says underwriters want fleets to go beyond tracking drivers "to actively coaching them." IOA suggests 360-degree cameras that can show who really caused a crash, including staged ones.

6. Close the gaps nobody talks about.

Two small coverage gaps can turn into big bills:

  • Bobtail coverage for owner-operators. A white paper from the International Risk Management Institute (IRMI) explains that courts split on when a truck is being used "in the business" of the carrier. Make sure your owner-operators' non-trucking coverage fits how their trucks are really used.
  • Additional insured wording in leases. An IRMI article on a federal appeals case shows that wording covering your "operations" can protect a landlord more broadly than wording that covers only your "use" of the space. If you lease docks or a terminal, read that wording.

7. Watch your theft risk.

RPS reports that cargo theft losses were about $131.6 million in the first quarter of 2026, even though the number of thefts fell. California had 36% of reported thefts. Thieves pose as drivers, steal identities and use double brokering. Confirm every pickup change by calling a number you already know. IOA adds that truck parking shortages feed theft. A $200 million federal fund for truck parking will help over time.

The bottom line

You cannot fix the courts. You cannot set the price of steel or the size of a jury award. But the gap between a strong fleet and a weak one keeps growing, and you decide which side of that gap you are on. Good data, quick claim reports, careful hiring and clean paperwork are the things that move your bill.

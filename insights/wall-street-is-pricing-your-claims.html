Wall Street Is Pricing Your Claims

By Michael Havemeyer, TRS, TCS · Oct. 29, 2026

Insurance, insolvencies and oil reset freight's risk math. The market now prices risk, not just revenue.

Two large publicly traded freight companies posted strong third quarters, and investors still sent their shares sharply lower because of insurance and claims costs. At the same time, small-carrier bankruptcies are piling up, diesel is expected to stay high into 2027, and new research from ATRI and Allianz shows fuel, litigation and insolvency risk at the top of the list. If you own, run or finance a trucking or logistics business, this is the time to look hard at what your risk profile is costing you.

Wall Street is now pricing claims risk

  • ATRI's 2026 Top Industry Issues survey (3,000+ stakeholders) ranks Lawsuit Abuse Reform #2 and Insurance Cost/Availability #4.
  • ATRI found liability premiums rose 18.2% from 2021 to 2024 while crash involvement fell 2.6%, with 27% to 50% increases for fleets under 100 trucks and a 45% jump in the $10 to $15 million excess layer.
  • ATRI also expects Montgomery to push brokers to require much higher carrier limits and to move from contingent to primary coverage.

Why it matters: Claims are no longer a back-office line item; they show up in company value. As brokerage consolidates into fewer, larger players that set carrier insurance and vetting rules, carriers with documented safety and clean loss histories will be the ones that get the freight and the better terms.

Bankruptcies rising: your customers' credit is your risk

  • Allianz Research (Oct. 27) projects U.S. business insolvencies up 12% in 2026 to 27,700 cases and rising to 28,800 in 2027, the highest level since 2011. It says fewer than one in two U.S. SMEs is profitable.
  • ATRI puts non-fuel operating costs at a record $1.854 per mile, with 2025 truckload and refrigerated margins below 1%.

Why it matters: Allianz warns that a high plateau of insolvencies means extended non-payment and supply-chain disruption risk, and says companies need to monitor key buyers and suppliers. Carrier failures hit shippers and brokers with service and credit losses. Trade credit insurance can help companies protect receivables and keep working capital moving when a customer can't pay.

Fuel is trucking's #1 concern, and relief isn't close

  • Fuel prices are ATRI's #1 industry concern for 2026, up 17 spots, and the first time drivers have ranked fuel as their top issue. ATRI cites the EIA view that diesel would not return to 2025 levels until 2028 at the soonest.

My take: wars light the match, but persistent inflation is also a vote on fiscal credibility. When government debt keeps climbing without a believable plan to pay for it, prices and rates stay higher for longer. Small fleets with thin margins feel that first.

Why it matters: Fuel surcharge gaps and spot lags turn diesel spikes into cash-flow problems. Budget for a range, tighten fuel controls, and watch customers and subcontractors whose working capital is stretched.

Regulators tighten the screws

  • ATRI reports CSA rose to the #3 industry concern, its highest since 2015, as brokers look for documented proof they hired the safest carriers after Montgomery.
  • ATRI's September 2026 telematics study surveyed 111 motor carriers. More than 90% already use the core safety KPIs: critical events, hard braking, speeding and harsh turning/cornering. Maintenance KPIs are the least used category.

Why it matters: Your CSA profile is now a sales document. After Montgomery, safety has to be provable. The data most fleets already collect can document safe operations for brokers and underwriters, and ATRI flags preventive maintenance and diagnostics as untapped value. That supports the case for lower total cost of risk.

Final thought

The market now prices risk, not just revenue. Safety and operations lower your total cost of risk. Documented safety data, verified hiring and training records, current equipment values, disciplined fuel controls and protected receivables are what separate the companies investors and underwriters reward from the ones they punish. Insurance is the last line of defense; the best programs start with how you run the business.

When Fuel Rises, the Conversation Shifts to Total Cost of Risk

By Michael Havemeyer, TRS, TCS · LinkedIn post, about Oct. 29, 2026

Oil is back above May levels. Why that matters for fleets, and why every claim and deductible hurts more when margins tighten.

Oil is back above May levels. That matters for fleets. Brent crude climbed to $114.92 per barrel, while average gasoline prices reached $4.29 per gallon (as reported by The Produce Wire).

Why it matters

  • Fuel is typically a fleet's second-largest expense behind labor.
  • Rising fuel costs squeeze margins, especially for carriers locked into contract rates.
  • When profits get tighter, every accident, claim, deductible, and uninsured exposure hurts more.

For fleet owners and CFOs, this is when the conversation shifts from premium cost to total cost of risk.
